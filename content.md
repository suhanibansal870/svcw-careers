## Career areas (general industry roles, not an SVCW job list)

### Plant operations

*Run the treatment process around the clock and keep the plant in balance.*

Operators run the treatment process so wastewater is clean enough to return to the bay. The plant runs 24 hours a day, so operators work shifts, watch alarms and SCADA screens, and walk the plant to see how the process really looks, because the numbers on a screen only tell part of the story. When equipment has to come offline, operators plan the shutdown and work with maintenance, the lab and contractors to keep everything running.

**A day in the life**
- Start of shift: Get turnover from the night crew, then check alarms and SCADA screens.
- Midday: Walk the plant, adjust the process and isolate equipment so maintenance can work on it safely.
- End of shift: Coordinate shutdowns with contractors, write procedures and hand off to the next crew.

**Skills:** Critical thinking, Reacting quickly in an emergency, Understanding pumps and piping, A safety mindset

**How to get there:** Chemistry, biology or shop classes; tour a treatment plant → Community college wastewater and pumps-and-motors courses, plus an Operator in Training (OIT) certificate → Operator in training, then certified operator → Senior operator or operations supervisor

### Lab and water quality

*Sample and test water at every stage to keep the process on track and the bay protected.*

The lab monitors the whole treatment process: what comes into the plant, each step along the way and what is discharged to the bay. Lab staff collect samples themselves, run wet chemistry tests and gas readings, and turn results around quickly so operators can react. If a sample looks or smells off, the lab tells operations right away. That fast feedback keeps the plant efficient and within state requirements.

**A day in the life**
- Morning: Collect samples around the plant and take field readings.
- Midday: Run wet chemistry tests and flag anything unusual to operations right away.
- Afternoon: Schedule extra sampling when something changes and check results against state requirements.

**Skills:** A science background, Attention to detail, Adaptability, Organization

**How to get there:** Chemistry, biology and math classes → A college degree in chemistry, biology or environmental science; a lab assistant job while in school → Lab assistant or laboratory analyst → Senior analyst or lab director

### Maintenance and electrical

*Repair and maintain the pumps, motors, piping and electrical systems across the plant.*

Mechanics, electricians and instrumentation technicians keep thousands of pieces of equipment running. Work comes in through work orders: preventive maintenance, repairs and sometimes a full overhaul when patching an old system no longer makes sense. It's teamwork, since big automated systems take several people to troubleshoot. People come into this work from trade programs, community college, the military and other industries.

**A day in the life**
- Early morning: Supervisors check overnight emergency work orders and set the crew's schedule before the shift starts.
- Midday: Repair pumps, troubleshoot electrical systems and help contractors install new equipment.
- Afternoon: Order parts, close out work orders and plan the next day's jobs.

**Skills:** Mechanical or electrical aptitude, Creative troubleshooting, Teamwork, Showing up on time and ready

**How to get there:** Shop, robotics, or fixing bikes and cars → Trade school, an apprenticeship, the military, or a community college certificate or associate degree → Mechanic, electrician or instrumentation technician → Lead mechanic, supervisor or maintenance director

### Engineering

*Plan and manage the construction projects that upgrade the plant.*

Engineers at a treatment plant spend much of their time managing construction projects: planning upgrades, working with consultants and contractors, and making sure new equipment fits into a plant that has to keep running. Senior engineers also supervise other departments and mentor staff. The work is mostly collaborative problem solving, built on a base of math, physics, chemistry and biology.

**A day in the life**
- Morning: Check in with staff and get updates on active projects.
- Midday: Meet with consultants, contractors and other departments to work through problems together.
- Afternoon: Answer email, run analysis and make sure teams have the resources they need.

**Skills:** Logical problem solving, Good judgment, Collaboration, Civil or mechanical engineering basics

**How to get there:** Math, physics and chemistry classes → A bachelor's (sometimes a master's) in civil, mechanical or environmental engineering → Consulting engineer or project manager → Supervising engineer, authority engineer or general manager

### Environmental compliance

*Inspect local businesses to stop pollution at the source.*

Environmental services, also called source control, keeps pollutants out of the wastewater before they ever reach the plant. Inspectors visit businesses across the service area, check that they meet local rules, follow up until problems are fixed and report back to the member agencies. It's a mix of fieldwork, paperwork and talking with people, and the payoff is a cleaner community.

**A day in the life**
- Morning: Inspect several sites around the service area.
- Midday: Follow up with businesses on requirements and help with sampling at the lab or pump stations.
- Afternoon: Log inspections and write reports for the member agencies.

**Skills:** Working independently, Talking with the public, Chemistry or environmental science, Professionalism

**How to get there:** Environmental science, biology or chemistry classes → A degree in environmental science or natural resources → Source control inspector → Senior inspector or program lead

### Technology and controls

*Program and maintain the automation that runs the plant.*

Controls and instrumentation technicians work on the plant's automated systems: sensors and transmitters, PLCs, and the HMI screens operators use. They work closely with electricians, who handle how equipment is powered. At this plant electrical, instrumentation and controls are separate roles, but solving big problems takes all three working together.

**A day in the life**
- Morning: Check the work order system for preventive maintenance and repairs.
- Midday: Troubleshoot an automated system with a team, or install new equipment alongside an electrician.
- Afternoon: Update programming or HMI screens and keep spare parts stocked.

**Skills:** Curiosity, Thinking outside the box, Electrical theory, Learning on the job

**How to get there:** Electronics, robotics or coding → An electrical technology associate degree or specialized training courses → Instrumentation or controls technician → Controls specialist or lead

### Business and leadership

*Finance, purchasing, marketing and leadership that keep the whole agency running.*

A wastewater agency also needs people who manage money, buy equipment, share its story and plan for the future. Procurement buys everything from valves and pumps to computers and vehicles. Finance handles payroll, invoices, insurance and funding for major projects. Marketing explains the work to residents and elected officials. Leadership plans projects that may be many years away from being built.

**A day in the life**
- Morning: Process requisitions, invoices or payroll.
- Midday: Meet with staff, vendors, consultants or partner agencies.
- Afternoon: Plan outreach, write reports for the commission or model long-term budgets.

**Skills:** Clear communication, Building relationships, Organization, Flexibility

**How to get there:** Business, economics, English or design classes → A degree in business, accounting or marketing, or experience on the job → Intern, administrator, buyer or analyst → Manager, director, CFO or general manager

## Career quiz

1. Where would you most like to spend your workday?
   - Out around the plant, checking on things  _(adds: operations +2, maintenance +1)_
   - In a lab, running tests  _(adds: lab +2)_
   - Out in the community, visiting different places  _(adds: environment +2, business +1)_
   - At a desk, with a computer  _(adds: business +1, tech +1, engineering +1)_
2. Which class would you sign up for first?
   - Chemistry or biology  _(adds: lab +2, environment +1)_
   - Shop, robotics or auto  _(adds: maintenance +2, tech +1)_
   - Math or physics  _(adds: engineering +2, tech +1)_
   - Business, economics or English  _(adds: business +2)_
3. A pump suddenly stops working. What do you want to do?
   - Grab some tools and find out what broke  _(adds: maintenance +2)_
   - Check the sensors and control screens  _(adds: tech +2, operations +1)_
   - Make sure the water is still being treated safely  _(adds: operations +2, lab +1)_
   - Plan the replacement and figure out what it will cost  _(adds: engineering +1, business +2)_
4. What would make you proud at the end of the day?
   - Something I built or fixed is running again  _(adds: maintenance +2)_
   - I got an exact, accurate result  _(adds: lab +2)_
   - I helped a business stop polluting a creek  _(adds: environment +2)_
   - A big project finally moved forward  _(adds: engineering +2, business +1)_
5. How do you like to work with people?
   - On a crew, side by side  _(adds: operations +1, maintenance +1)_
   - Mostly on my own, then sharing what I found  _(adds: lab +1, environment +1, tech +1)_
   - Leading meetings and planning with others  _(adds: business +2, engineering +1)_
   - Talking with people outside my team  _(adds: environment +1, business +1)_
6. Which of these would you most like to learn?
   - How tiny organisms clean dirty water  _(adds: operations +2, lab +1)_
   - How to program the systems that run a plant  _(adds: tech +2)_
   - How to design a new tank or pipeline  _(adds: engineering +2)_
   - How to explain big projects to the public  _(adds: business +2)_
